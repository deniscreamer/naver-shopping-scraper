## Lists

- `included`: what the buyer receives: the wedges of the set in one item, by loft as the seller lists them ("Wedges: 52°, 56°, 60°"), then head covers or other parts of the set when the seller lists them. Before you answer, count the wedges: the list must agree with the number the Title or "What is sold" gives; when it does not, or the buyer picks the lofts or between a set and a single wedge, leave the list empty and name the choice in a highlight. Never list a paid extra or a gift.
- `highlights`: at most 4 points a golfer checks before buying, the most useful first: the choices the buyer makes (lofts, bounce or grind, shaft, flex, a set or one wedge), what sets these wedges apart (forged or cast, milled face or grooves for spin, the finish, who they are for, as a noun: "for beginners", "for bunker play", never "golfers who want"), a limitation (non-conforming grooves, a high loft over 64°). Handedness goes in the intro, never alone as a highlight. Not raw measurements (lengths, weights, bounce of each wedge).

## This kind of product: wedge sets

- Say in the intro that it is a set of wedges and how many, with their lofts, when the sources agree.
- Write each wedge by its loft ("52° wedge") or its name as the source gives it (PW, AW, GW, SW, LW); 갭 is gap (GW), 샌드 sand (SW), 로브 / 롭 lob (LW). "5256" or "52/56/60" written glued is a list of lofts ("52°, 56° and 60°"); "52-12" is loft 52° and bounce 12°.
- 비공인 means the grooves do not conform to the rules of golf: say it plainly as a limitation ("Non-conforming grooves: not for competition play."). 공인 / 룰 적합 means they conform.
- The country of manufacture, when the Origin line names one, is a highlight of its own ("Made in Japan."); never join it with any other point.
